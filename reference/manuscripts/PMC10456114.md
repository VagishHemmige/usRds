### PMC10456114

[Pressure Ulcer Diagnosis Is Associated with Increased Mortality in Patients with End-Stage Renal Disease: A Retrospective Study.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10456114/) · 2023 · doi:10.3390/life13081713

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** 1,526,366 adults starting dialysis in 2005–2018; person-level ever-recorded pressure ulcer and mortality from dialysis initiation through 2019. [reported Methods; Results]
- **Sources:** USRDS Medicare diagnosis claims and CMS-2728 initial modality/access; excludes missing demographic/modality/access information. [reported Methods]
- **Data needs:** at least one pressure-ulcer claim; propensity-weighted Cox models and backward selection with demographic/comorbidity adjustment. Code lists are supplementary and unverified. [reported Methods]
- **Pitfalls:** an ever-diagnosed exposure with follow-up beginning before diagnosis can misassign prediagnosis time and introduce immortal-time bias; propensity weighting alone does not repair timing. Continuous observable claims coverage is not specified. Sections 2.2 and 2.4 describe different claim-source breadth. Large sample size cannot remove these biases. [reported Methods; Limitations; inference]
- **Locators:** complete main prose, especially sections 2.2–2.5 and 4.4; tables/supplements unread. Preserve diagnosis dates and distinguish baseline disease from later incident disease. This is a cautionary design example, not evidence to import a fixed ever-diagnosed mortality predictor.
