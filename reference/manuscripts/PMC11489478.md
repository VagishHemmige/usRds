### PMC11489478

[Elevated Ambient Temperature Associated With Increased Cardiovascular Disease-Risk Among Patients on Hemodialysis.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11489478/) · 2024 · doi:10.1016/j.ekir.2024.07.015

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** 670,491 Medicare-primary in-center HD patients, 2011–2016, after three months on dialysis; warm-season ZIP/day outcome counts. [reported Methods; Results]
- **Sources:** USRDS claims/deaths and last-visited dialysis-clinic ZIP linked to Daymet v4 daily temperatures in the contiguous US. [reported Methods]
- **Data needs:** hospital cause groups use first three diagnoses; ED groups use all recorded diagnoses. Conditional Poisson case-crossover analyses stratify by weekday, month, year and statistical area, adjust humidity, and examine nonlinear same-day and three-day cumulative lags. [reported Methods]
- **Pitfalls:** clinic ZIP is a proxy for personal exposure; spatial grids must be aggregated before event linkage. Primary-only versus all-code sensitivity analyses change classification. Counts of visits are not counts of patients; recurring encounters must retain their dates. Region-specific percentile contrasts need their own temperature values. [reported Methods; Discussion; inference]
- **Locators:** complete main prose, including repeated retrieved sections; tables, diagnosis supplement and equation/figure images unverified. Negative-control exposure randomization is useful validation, but it does not establish causal identification or validate an incorrectly constructed event denominator.
