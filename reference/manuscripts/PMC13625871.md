### PMC13625871

[Neighborhood Disadvantage and Dementia Risk Among Older Patients With Kidney Failure: Differences by Race/Ethnicity and Urbanicity.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13625871/) · 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.xkme.2026.101479

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** USRDS patients aged ≥55 initiating dialysis in 2003–2021, Medicare-primary A/B; person and ZIP-level disadvantage. [reported Methods]
- **Sources:** CMS-2728/Patients, multiple Medicare claim settings, external neighborhood index combining ACS 2015–2019 and other sources. Tract measures are population-weighted to ZIP. [reported Methods]
- **Data needs:** complete-case analysis; one-year prevalent-dementia lookback only for ages ≥66. Follow dialysis start to diagnosis, death, transplant, coverage end or end-2021. Cause-specific primary models; Fine–Gray and two-distinct-date diagnosis sensitivity analyses. [reported Methods]
- **Pitfalls:** static contemporary neighborhood scores do not reconstruct historical residence or exposure. Differential prebaseline ascertainment by age can leave prevalent dementia among younger entrants. Kaplan–Meier estimates labeled cumulative incidence do not account for competing death; distinguish them from the sensitivity estimand. Confirmation uses the first code date, with no bounded interval stated. [reported Methods; Limitations; inference]
- **Locators:** complete main text; detailed diagnosis lists, tables and supplements unread. External-index thresholds and missingness exclusions must remain separate from general USRDS variable recoding.
