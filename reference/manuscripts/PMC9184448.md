### PMC9184448

[Costs in the Year Following Deceased Donor Kidney Transplantation: Relationships With Renal Function and Graft Failure.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9184448/) · 2022 · doi:10.3389/ti.2022.10422

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** USRDS cost analysis; adult single-organ deceased-donor kidney transplants, 2012–2015, follow-up through 2016; first transplant in the study window and recipient-month. [reported Methods]
- **Files and fields:** registry transplant/creatinine data and Medicare A/B claims; costs start after discharge from the transplant hospitalization. [reported Data Sources; Cost Variable Definitions]
- **Data needs:** eGFR at discharge, six and twelve months, using creatinine within two weeks of each time; compare graft-failure-centered costs with a composite control curve weighted to corresponding time since transplant. Costs adjusted to 2019 dollars; gamma/log models. [reported Methods]
- **Pitfalls:** payment fields, inflation index and claim-proration rules are unspecified. Missing-creatinine exclusions and treatment of death affect selection and denominators. A composite average control trajectory is not individual risk-set matching. Dialysis is included largely in outpatient costs and cannot simply be added again as a separate category.
