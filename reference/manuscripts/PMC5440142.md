### PMC5440142

[Explaining trends and variation in timing of dialysis initiation in the United States.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5440142/) · 2017 · doi:10.1097/md.0000000000006911

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** USRDS incident adult dialysis cohort, 1995–2012; patient eGFR clustered by physician signing CMS-2728. [reported Methods]
- **Sources:** two CMS-2728 versions, CMS-2744 facility information, fixed 2000 Census county context and RUCA. Exclude preemptive transplant and missing analysis variables. [reported Methods]
- **Data needs:** calculate MDRD eGFR from predialysis creatinine; bound values at 2 and 20 mL/min/1.73 m². Sequential mixed models partition residual variation. Added 2005-form items enter restricted-era sensitivity analyses; multiple imputation covers only 2006–2009. [reported Methods]
- **Pitfalls:** winsorized eGFR is not raw eGFR; historical equation/calibration must be recorded rather than replaced silently. A form-signing physician need not have made the dialysis decision. Fixed-area context and changing form coverage complicate longitudinal comparisons. Variance equations are missing from the retrieved text and require original rendering before implementation. [reported Methods; Limitations; retrieval gap]
- **Locators:** complete prose main text; equations, tables/images/supplements unverified. Residual variation does not by itself establish causal physician preference or a validated dialysis-timing metric.
