### PMC12691873

[A Multilevel Spatial Survival Analysis of Patients in Texas with End-Stage Renal Disease.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12691873/) · 2025 · doi:10.3390/healthcare13233028

Reading: **MT**, 2026-11-15. Main-text reading; figures as images and supplements not inspected; tables not comprehensively checked unless stated.

- **Role / population / unit:** Texas adults starting kidney-failure treatment, 2009–2018; person nested in county, with county spatial eigenvectors. [reported Methods]
- **Sources:** USRDS clinical/geographic data and ACS county characteristics. Models compare Cox, county random intercepts and Moran eigenvector filtering. [reported Methods]
- **Data needs:** binary county adjacency, aligned county identifiers and residual spatial diagnostics; 19 selected eigenvectors describe spatial structure. [reported Methods; Results]
- **Pitfalls:** cause of death is used as a mortality predictor, introducing outcome leakage. Transplant counts appear as fixed covariates despite potentially occurring later, risking immortal time. Adding transplant status does not automatically fix informative censoring. Spatial fit improvement cannot repair these temporal design problems. [reported Methods; inference]
- **Locators:** complete prose main text; mathematical notation incompletely rendered and tables/maps unread. Retain as a spatial-method comparison with substantial design cautions, not a validated survival template. County-level associations and fitted residual surfaces do not identify causal intervention targets.
